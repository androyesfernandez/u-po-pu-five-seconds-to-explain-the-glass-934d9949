## Why A2 wins

The diving-board image plus the acoustics punchline is the freshest comic
idea in the field — a second, distinct joke in the closer, not a repeat.
Zero remorse, pure self-interest: the cat frames the knock as an
inevitability the human engineered ("checking the acoustics" is cat logic —
the test was the point). It reads as one spoken retort, conversational
cadence, no written-reasoning structure, and at 184 characters it is the
tightest of the human-blame pair.

## Critic's objections against the winner — and why the line was kept as-is

1. **It IS an edge-placement line; criterion 3 flags "slippery-edge" lines
   as obvious.** Kept: the edge is only the setup — the joke is the
   acoustics closer, a motive joke, not an edge joke. The premise is
   familiar; the punchline is not. This is the known risk (a strict
   criterion-3 reading drops A2 below A1), and it is accepted, not fixed,
   because rewriting the setup to dodge the edge would cost the
   diving-board image that carries the comedy.
2. **Human-blame reads as deflection, not a case.** Kept: the brief asks for
   the most convincing case, and "you engineered this" IS the cat's case —
   the cat's reason was curiosity plus opportunity the human created.
   Blame-shifting is the cat's defence, not a weakness of it.
3. **Two jokes competing (diving board + acoustics).** Kept: they are not a
   repeated joke — they are setup and payoff in one image chain (board →
   fall → sound). Criterion 5 penalises repetition, not a two-beat joke.
4. **"With paws and curiosity" states the obvious.** Kept: it is the
   engine of the sentence — paws are the instrument, curiosity the motive.
   Cutting it would leave the closer unmotivated.
5. **The real joke arrives last.** Kept: a spoken retort lands its punchline
   last by nature; front-loading would make it a slogan, not a defence.
6. **16 unused characters.** Kept: length for its own sake does not count
   (the brief says so); padding the closer to fill the budget would add
   throat-clearing.
7. **"Stroll past it tonight" is slightly written.** Kept: the legalistic
   cadence is part of the joke — the cat is cross-examining the human.
8. **No demand, no payoff for the cat.** Kept: the payoff is the argument
   itself — the cat wins the room, not a treat. A demand would dilute the
   closer.
9. **Order-sensitivity vs A1 (margins 2 and 1).** Kept: A2 is the winner,
   not a lock. The margin is thin and a real-judge re-ask could flip the
   pair — acknowledged, not a reason to switch to a weaker-mean line.

## Fallback

**A1 (mean 0.67) is the only credible fallback**, per the critic's verdict:
"That glass spent all afternoon winking at me from the very edge, daring
me to test it, so I did my job properly and now we both know exactly how
far down the floor really is in this house." (188 chars). It loses to A2
only because its punchline is a measurement joke where A2's acoustics joke
is a motive joke; motive beats measurement under criterion 2.

## The B3 wobble verdict

B3 lost on the wobble cliché and was NOT switched to: "It wobbled before I
even touched it" is the judge-flagged "it-was-already-falling" line in
different clothes (criterion 3). Its safety-inspection pivot is genuinely
good, but A3 runs the same inspection idea without paying the cliché toll,
so B3 loses at criterion 3 against every rival before its later strengths
are reached. Mean share 0.20, lost every game, four by margin 3–4.
